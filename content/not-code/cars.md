I have been around cars for as long as I have been around computers, and for longer than
I have been paid for either.

In 2019 I spent a week at [Fiskens](https://fiskens.com/) in London, helping run the
showroom and cleaning historic cars, which is a more instructive job than it sounds: you
learn a car's condition with your hands before you learn its history. In 2023 and 2024 I
worked the RM Sotheby's sales in London and Monaco, on the ATK desk with prospective
buyers and on the logistics of getting the cars onto the floor. Auctions are where the
stories and the money meet, and watching which one moves the other is an education.

Before both of those, my EPQ was a research project titled *The Influence of Cars on
Society*. I was seventeen. I have been writing a version of that argument ever since, most
recently as a book, [The Art of Automotive Elegance](/projects/automotive-elegance/), and
as [The Bentley Registry](/projects/bentley-registry/).

## The ones I keep coming back to

**Bugatti Type 35A.** The most successful racing car ever built, and it happens to be
beautiful, which almost never goes together. Everything after it is a footnote to the
proportions.

**Alfa Romeo 8C.** The 1930s at their most confident. A car built by people who had not
yet been told that engineering and art were separate disciplines.

**Mercedes-Benz 300SL Gullwing.** A spaceframe that forced a design decision, and the
design decision became the most recognisable door in history. Constraint doing the work
of imagination.

**Bentley.** Less a single car than a whole argument about what fast should feel like:
unhurried, heavy, and entirely unbothered. The pre-war cars are the ones I research.

**Ferrari.** Included honestly rather than fashionably. Some of it is noise. The rest is
the only marque that has been continuously interesting for seventy-five years.
